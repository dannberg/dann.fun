Japan: A Cultural Journey

Tokyo · Nagano · Kyoto — for Dann & Avi
Dec 23 → Dec 31 · 9 Days

The Route

This itinerary moves north before south: you'll land in Tokyo, settle in for two days, then take the Shinkansen north to Nagano for the snow monkeys and an overnight onsen stay. From there, head south to Kyoto for three nights of temples, kaiseki, and a Nara day trip, before returning to Tokyo for New Year's Eve — with a stop at Iron Heart along the way.

The pace is busy but with breathing room built in: mornings are structured, afternoons leave space to wander, and evenings are anchored by great meals.

JR Pass verdict: With Tokyo → Nagano round-trip (~¥16,000) plus Tokyo → Kyoto round-trip (~¥27,000) plus local JR rides, your total JR fares will approach ~¥50,000. A 7-day JR Pass (¥50,000) is borderline — consider individual tickets via SmartEX for flexibility and Nozomi access, or grab the pass if you value convenience. Either way, get Suica/Pasmo on your phones for local transit.

Golden Gai & Omoide Yokocho

Shake off the jet lag with a walk through the neon-lit alleyways of Shinjuku. Start at Omoide Yokocho ("Memory Lane") for yakitori under the smoke, then wander the tiny bars of Golden Gai. No agenda — just absorb the energy.

02
Wednesday, Dec 24
Classic Tokyo & Christmas Eve Omakase
Tokyo — Asakusa · Ginza · Shibuya
Morning

Senso-ji & Asakusa

Visit Tokyo's oldest temple early before crowds build. Walk through Nakamise-dori for snacks (melon pan, senbei). The winter morning light on the five-story pagoda is gorgeous.

Culture
Midday

Tsukiji Outer Market

Hop on the Ginza Line to Tsukiji for a seafood lunch grazing session — tamagoyaki, fresh uni, grilled scallops. The outer market is still thriving and very much worth visiting.

Food
Afternoon

Meiji Shrine & Harajuku

Walk the forested path to Meiji Jingu for a moment of stillness. Then contrast it with a stroll through Takeshita-dori and the Omotesando designer boulevard. Pop into any shops that catch your eye.

Culture
Evening

Christmas Eve Omakase

Christmas Eve is a big date night in Japan — lean into it. Book a splurge-worthy omakase: Sushi Ryo (Ginza, 8-seat counter, incredible Edomae style), Kyubey (Ginza institution), or Sushi Koshikawa (affordable luxury in Kichijoji). Book well in advance via Omakase.in or TableAll.

Splurge Meal
🌃
After dinner: Walk through Marunouchi illuminations (~775,000 LEDs along Naka-dori street). It's peak Christmas magic.
03
Thursday, Dec 25
Snow Monkeys & Onsen
Tokyo → Nagano → Yamanouchi
Early Morning

Hokuriku Shinkansen to Nagano

Catch an early bullet train from Tokyo Station to Nagano (~80 min). From Nagano Station, take the Nagaden Express Bus to the Snow Monkey Park stop (~40 min).

Transit
Late Morning

Jigokudani Monkey Park

Walk the forest trail (~30 min) to reach the hot spring pool. Late December is prime season — expect snow-dusted macaques soaking contentedly in steaming water. Spend 1.5–2 hours watching the troop. Dress warmly (expect ~20°F / -5°C). Sturdy, waterproof shoes are a must.

NatureCulture
Afternoon

Shibu Onsen Check-in

Head to nearby Shibu Onsen, a charming hot spring village with narrow stone lanes and wooden ryokan. Check in and spend the afternoon doing the famous nine public bathhouse circuit (ryokan guests get free access). Each bath is said to cure a different ailment.

CultureNature
Evening

Kaiseki dinner at ryokan

Most ryokan include a multi-course kaiseki dinner. Let the staff bring dish after dish to your room while you sit in your yukata, warm from the baths. This will be one of the most memorable nights of the trip.

Splurge Meal
♨️
Stay: Ryokan in Shibu Onsen — Look at Kanaguya (historic, stunning), Shibu Hotel, or Yamazakiya. Book early — Christmas week fills up.
04
Friday, Dec 26
Zenko-ji & Transit to Kyoto
Nagano → Kyoto
Morning

Zenko-ji Temple, Nagano

Head back to Nagano City for a visit to one of Japan's most important Buddhist temples, founded in the 7th century. Walk the atmospheric approach road lined with shops selling oyaki (savory steamed buns) and local sake. Don't miss the pitch-black tunnel beneath the altar — touching the "Key to Paradise" in total darkness is unforgettable.

Culture
Midday

Shinkansen to Kyoto

Take the Hokuriku Shinkansen from Nagano to Kanazawa, then transfer to the Thunderbird limited express to Kyoto (~4 hours total). Alternatively, backtrack to Tokyo and take the Tokaido Shinkansen to Kyoto (~2+2 hours). Settle into your Kyoto hotel.

Transit
Evening

Pontocho Alley

Stroll the narrow lantern-lit Pontocho alley along the Kamo River. Pick a riverside restaurant for dinner — many have outdoor terrace seating even in winter. The atmospheric glow of this historic geisha district is extraordinary at night.

FoodCulture
🏯
Stay: Central Kyoto — 3 nights. Consider The Hotel Granvia (above the station), Kyoto Hotel Okura (Kawaramachi), or a machiya townhouse rental for atmosphere.
05
Saturday, Dec 27
Kyoto's Greatest Hits
Kyoto — Eastern Higashiyama
Early Morning

Fushimi Inari Taisha

Get there at opening to walk the seemingly endless vermillion torii gates with minimal crowds. You don't need to hike the full loop — the first 20 minutes up gives you the iconic shots. Early morning winter light filtering through thousands of gates is otherworldly.

Culture
Morning

Kiyomizu-dera

One of Kyoto's most celebrated temples, perched on a hillside with sweeping views of the city. The wooden stage juts out over the valley — in winter, the bare maple branches frame the view beautifully. Walk down through the charming Higashiyama Sannen-zaka and Ninen-zaka lanes below.

Culture
Afternoon

Gion & Tea

Wander through the traditional Gion district. Pop into a tea house for matcha and wagashi (traditional sweets). Walk along Shirakawa canal — the old wooden machiya houses and willow trees are postcard-perfect. If you're lucky, you might spot a maiko heading to an evening engagement.

CultureFood
Evening

Kaiseki in Gion

This is your Kyoto fine dining night. Kaiseki is Kyoto's highest culinary art — a multi-course meal that follows the seasons. Look at Gion Sasaki (2 Michelin stars), Kikunoi Honten (3 stars), or Gion Maruyama for something more approachable but still exceptional.

Splurge Meal
🍵
Tip: Michelin kaiseki spots book weeks or months out. Reserve ASAP — holiday week is peak season. Use Omakase.in, Pocket Concierge, or your hotel concierge.
06
Sunday, Dec 28
Nara Day Trip — Deer & the Great Buddha
Day trip: Kyoto → Nara → Kyoto
Morning

Train to Nara

Take the Kintetsu Limited Express from Kyoto (~35 min) to Nara. It's compact and walkable — perfect for a day trip. Head straight to Nara Park where over 1,000 sacred deer roam freely.

Transit
Late Morning

Todai-ji Temple

Home to the Great Buddha (Daibutsu) — a 15-meter-tall bronze statue housed inside the largest wooden building in the world. The scale is staggering even if you've seen photos. Walk through the Nandaimon Gate with its fierce guardian statues on the way in.

Culture
Afternoon

Kasuga Taisha & Naramachi

Walk the path lined with thousands of stone lanterns to Kasuga Grand Shrine. Then explore the old merchant quarter of Naramachi — narrow streets with well-preserved townhouses, small museums, and craft shops. Great for picking up souvenirs.

Culture
Evening

Return to Kyoto

Head back to Kyoto for a relaxed evening. Try a local izakaya near your hotel or explore Nishiki Market (the "Kitchen of Kyoto") for street food — pickles, grilled mochi, and seasonal treats.

Food
🦌
Nara tip: Buy shika senbei (deer crackers, ¥200) and prepare for the deer to mob you enthusiastically. They bow for treats — it's charming.
07
Monday, Dec 29
Arashiyama & Golden Pavilion
Kyoto — West Side
Morning

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

Take the JR San-in Line to Saga-Arashiyama. Walk through the towering bamboo forest early — the sound of the wind through the stalks is meditative. Continue to Tenryu-ji temple and its stunning garden (a UNESCO World Heritage site).

CultureNature
Late Morning

Iwatayama Monkey Park

If you want a second monkey fix, climb the hill in Arashiyama for panoramic city views and more macaques — these are the same species as the snow monkeys but in a different setting.

Nature
Afternoon

Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

Head north to the iconic Golden Pavilion. The gold-leaf temple reflected in the mirror pond is breathtaking — and if there's any chance of snow, it becomes one of the most photographed scenes in all of Japan. Winter is the best season for a dusting of white on the golden roof.

Culture
Evening

Kyoto Station Ramen & Pack Up

For your last Kyoto evening, hit Kyoto Ramen Street on the 10th floor of the station building — a row of famous ramen shops from across Japan. Pick your style and slurp with abandon before packing for tomorrow's move.

Food
🎋
Last night in Kyoto. Consider a final evening stroll through Gion or along the Kamo River.

Tuesday, Dec 30
Iron Heart Pilgrimage & Tokyo Return
Kyoto → Tokyo → Hachioji → Shibuya
Morning

Shinkansen back to Tokyo

Take the Hikari Shinkansen from Kyoto to Tokyo (~2h 20min). Drop bags at your Tokyo hotel.

Transit
Midday

Iron Heart HQ — Hachioji

Take the JR Chuo Line from Shinjuku to Hachioji Station (~45 min), then a bus or taxi (~15 min) to the Iron Heart flagship at 733-19 Utsukimachi. Hours: Wed–Sun 11:00–18:00 (closed Mon/Tue, so Tuesday Dec 30 won't work — see note below). Browse their full range of heavyweight selvedge, try things on, and have them call you a taxi back to the station (~¥2,000).

Denim
Afternoon

Shibuya & Shimokitazawa

Back in central Tokyo, explore Shibuya Crossing, then take the Keio-Inokashira Line to Shimokitazawa — Tokyo's vintage clothing and thrift store paradise. Great for raw denim hunting at shops like Bears, Flamingo, and various secondhand spots.

DenimCulture
Evening

Pre–New Year's Eve dinner

Go for something fun and lively — a high-end yakitori spot like Birdland (Ginza) or an izakaya crawl through Ebisu Yokocho. Keep it casual since tomorrow is the main event.

Food

Wednesday, Dec 31
New Year's Eve & Departure
Tokyo
Morning

Toyosu Market (if your flight is late)

If you have time before heading to the airport, visit the Toyosu Fish Market for a final sushi breakfast. The tuna auction viewing gallery opens early. Otherwise, enjoy a leisurely hotel breakfast and soak in your last Tokyo morning.

Food
Midday

Last-minute shopping

Nakamise-dori (Asakusa) or the Ginza underground malls are great for last-minute gifts — matcha Kit-Kats, wagashi, tenugui cloths, chopsticks. Don Quijote in Shibuya for the chaotic souvenir experience.

Culture
Afternoon / Evening

Depart from Narita (NRT)

Take the Narita Express back to the airport. Allow at least 2.5 hours before your flight. If your flight is late evening, consider attending Joya no Kane — the 108 bell strikes at a temple for New Year's Eve. Senso-ji is the most accessible option.

Transit
✈️
If your flight is on Jan 1 instead: Spend NYE at Senso-ji or Meiji Shrine for Hatsumode (first shrine visit of the new year). It's crowded but electric — one of Japan's most important traditions.

Practical Notes

Getting Around

Add Suica to Apple Wallet before you land. It works on virtually all trains, buses, and convenience stores. For Shinkansen, download the SmartEX app to book bullet train seats from your phone.

Connectivity

Buy an eSIM before departure (Ubigi, Airalo, or Saily all work well in Japan). Activate it on the plane so you're connected at landing. Free WiFi in Japan is unreliable.

Weather

Tokyo: 40–50°F (5–10°C), dry and sunny. Nagano: 20–30°F (-5 to 0°C) with snow. Kyoto: 35–45°F (2–7°C). Pack layers, a warm coat, waterproof shoes for Nagano, and a compact umbrella.

Reservations

Book your omakase and kaiseki NOW — Christmas week is peak season. Use Omakase.in, TableAll, Pocket Concierge, or your hotel concierge. Ryokan in Shibu Onsen should also be booked ASAP.

Iron Heart Timing

The Hachioji store is open Wed–Sun, 11am–6pm. Your best window is Wed Dec 24 afternoon or Sat/Sun Dec 27–28. Alternatively, the Osaka store is a 30-min train ride from Kyoto.

Holiday Heads-Up

Dec 26–Jan 4 is Japan's New Year holiday. Some shops close Dec 31–Jan 1, but trains, convenience stores, and major attractions stay open. Temples will be packed on Jan 1.
